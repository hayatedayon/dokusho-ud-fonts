FONTLOG for Dokusho UD Mincho and Dokusho UD Gothic (読書 UD明朝・読書 UDゴシック)
==================================================================================

This file provides detailed information on the Dokusho UD fonts, following the
FONTLOG that SIL recommends for fonts under the Open Font License.

Basic Font Information
----------------------

Dokusho UD Mincho and Dokusho UD Gothic are fonts for reading books, in
horizontal and vertical text alike. They are Modified Versions of Morisawa's
BIZ UDMincho and BIZ UDGothic (SIL Open Font License 1.1).

Printed Japanese books set kana, kanji and punctuation on a full-width grid
(solid setting in the W3C Requirements for Japanese Text Layout), and Latin
letters in a proportional typeface. BIZ UD has full-width kana but monospaced
half-width Latin letters, and BIZ UDP has proportional Latin letters but also
proportional kana and punctuation, so neither sets text the way books do on its
own. Dokusho UD keeps everything of BIZ UD and takes only the Latin letters,
with their substitutions, from BIZ UDP of the same design.

ChangeLog
---------

2026-11-10 (hayatedayon) Dokusho UD 1.008
- Extended conservative Latin kerning to periods and commas after T, V, W,
  Y, P, F, r, y, v and w, including compatible accented forms. The values
  differ between Mincho and Gothic. Punctuation has its own lookup so
  zero-valued entries in earlier Class Pair subtables cannot mask it.
  Full-width, hwid, tabular and substituted vertical forms remain excluded;
  all declared pairs are checked with HarfBuzz and at least 1% em of ink space.
- Set hhea.lineGap and OS/2.sTypoLineGap to 1024 (0.5 em) in all four faces,
  so line-height: normal is 1.5 em. Ascenders, descenders,
  usWinAscent/usWinDescent and USE_TYPO_METRICS are unchanged. Existing
  documents using line-height: normal may change line spacing and pagination;
  applications that ignore these line gaps can behave differently.
- Added U+20BB7 (𠮷) using only original BIZ UD outlines: the upper 土 of 圭,
  translated without scaling, and the unchanged lower 口 of 吉. Its advances
  and vertical origin match 吉. The new outline has no hinting.
- Added U+3031/U+3032 (〱/〲) by joining the shared edges of 〳/〴 and 〵.
  Unicode specifies double-height vertical glyphs occupying two ideographic
  cells. Horizontal width is 1 em and vertical advance 2 em, also as in the
  locally available Noto Serif CJK JP; no Noto outlines are imported. The
  horizontal preview is uniformly half size inside one cell; vert/vrt2 select
  the original stroke size across two vertical cells. Both forms have no
  hinting. Their vertical bounds are checked in the two-cell frame, as for
  multi-em dashes, and horizontal forms stay within the existing line bounds.
- Added format 12 Unicode cmaps for the supplementary character. Existing
  cmap assignments and aalt alternatives are unchanged; ALWAYS_UDP still
  relies on locl.
- Added FreeType actual-size/4x/96px proofs for the new Japanese glyphs.
  Added an independent advisory CI workflow for native CoreText and
  DirectWrite glyph IDs, advances and PNGs, including explicit locl-off
  diagnostics for all 17 ALWAYS_UDP symbols. It passed on macOS 15.8.2 and
  Windows Server 2025. With both engines, ALWAYS_UDP takes BIZ UDP's widths
  in horizontal text by default, and locl=0 changes nothing, so it does not
  reproduce an environment without locl. In DirectWrite's vertical text,
  “ ” are set sideways at half width rather than as 〝 〟. Word, InDesign,
  e-readers, PDF and print remain unchecked (docs/PROOFING.md).

2026-11-09 (hayatedayon) Dokusho UD 1.007
- The double prime quotation mark 〞 (U+301E) is now 〝 mirrored about the
  center of the em, leaning the opposite way from 〝 as in other Japanese
  fonts such as Noto Serif CJK JP. It was 〟 raised, which leaned the same
  way as 〝. Its position, width and vertical form do not change. The new
  outline carries vertical-only hinting.
- In vertical text, “ ” now take the forms of the double minutes 〝 〟, and
  ‘ ’ one stroke of them, in a full-width body: JLReq treats “ ” ‘ ’ as
  horizontal-only and 〝 〟 as the vertical quotes. The text itself does not
  change, so copying and searching still find “ ” ‘ ’, and horizontal text is
  unchanged. Up to 1.006 they were half an em high beside the column, with
  ‘ ’ turned 180 degrees from other Japanese fonts. ss01 still selects BIZ
  UD's quotes in vertical text, with ‘ ’ in the corrected orientation.
  In Latin runs, as in the sideways word It’s, ‘ ’ take new forms turned 90
  degrees with the word, so that the word is not split. “ ” keep the minute
  forms there too: an opening “ after Japanese text belongs to the Japanese
  run, so turning only Latin runs would leave “Hello” with unmatched marks.
  The generated forms of ‘ ’ carry vertical-only hinting.
- check.py now detects mirrored or rotated glyphs and wrong digit mappings,
  horizontally and vertically, against a fixture recorded from Noto CJK JP.
- Added conservative Latin kerning for frequent pairs and their accented
  forms. Full-width, hwid, tabular and vertical forms are excluded.
- Excluded right-projecting carons (Tcaron and Lcaron) from left kerning
  classes, and reduced T-u kerning in both Mincho weights to avoid contact
  or near-contact. Both horizontal slices and closest outline-segment
  distances must have at least 1% em of ink space.
  Collision checks now expand every encoded GPOS kerning pair,
  including accented forms, rather than just a small representative sample.
- Aligned V/W/Y right-hand kerning classes with T's aeo/u split, so earlier
  zero-valued Class Pair subtables do not mask lower-case pairs. GPOS
  expansion now respects first-match rules including implicit class-zero
  values; HarfBuzz checks every declared pair and its accented variants
  against the configured adjustment, separately from collision checks.
- Added pnum to undo tnum, including when both are requested.
- Added named, opt-in ss01 (BIZ UD Japanese quotes) and ss02 (BIZ UDP Latin
  symbols excluding quotes), supporting horizontal and vertical text.
  ss02 uses dedicated copies of the Latin symbols and their vertical forms,
  retaining outlines, metrics, hinting and GDEF classes. Supplemental width
  lookups consume only these copies and restore the glyph that entered
  ss02. Ordinary locl outputs and the published hwid/fwid behavior remain
  unchanged. Shaping checks cover every symbol in Japanese/English,
  horizontal/vertical and mixed text contexts. An independent fixture from
  the published 1.006 checks widths, outlines and positioning for 956
  characters in eight width/language/orientation combinations per font.
  ss02 also supplies vrt2 forms for horizontally identical symbols such as
  superscripts 1-3 and, in two faces, cedilla. These forms are imported and
  hinted before style lookups are added; ordinary locl/vrt2 behavior stays
  unchanged. Checks cover default vert and explicit vert=0/vrt2 paths.
  Each style copy is matched to its corresponding BIZ UDP character/path,
  including otherwise unused copies with locl disabled, and rendered at
  8-24 ppem in three FreeType modes. ss01 vertical quote mappings now use
  recorded substitution metadata instead of glyph-name suffixes.
  Kerning inputs are also checked against every hwid/fwid/tnum/vert/vrt2
  output glyph, rather than only a few representative strings.
- Added ss03, an opt-in ruby kana trial for 20 hiragana/katakana characters,
  with slightly stronger strokes and 12% larger small kana. The existing
  ruby feature and default kana are unchanged. Trial vertical forms keep
  their original vertical origins and remain inside the line frame. Trial
  glyphs inherit palt/vpal adjustments from the original kana. The trials
  have no hinting; raster comparisons explicitly include this difference.
- Added vertical-only TrueType hinting to 56 generated numeric glyphs
  (superscripts, subscripts, numerators and denominators). Original 1-4
  superscripts retain their hinting; generated symbols remain unhinted.
  Each contour's extrema are touched with interpolated positions before IUP,
  so counters follow the same vertical transform as the outer contour.
  Generated digits now use additional, shared CVT top/bottom zones per role
  rather than independently rounding each digit. Denominators share baseline
  zero, including their fraction-adjacent forms. Original CVT entries and
  superscripts 1-4 are preserved. FreeType checks both outline and raster
  top/bottom alignment at 8-24 ppem, separately from counter distortion.
- Documented mutually exclusive palt/chws/halt and vpal/vchw/vhal settings.
  The specimen now preserves combined feature settings and prevents double
  trimming in proportional headings. Tests explicitly reproduce incompatible
  combinations and compare the safe settings with BIZ UDP.
- Added fixed reading/print proofs and actual-size/4x FreeType raster
  comparisons, generated in CI with a separate proof artifact. Added
  end-to-end checks for the new features, glyph contact, clipping and
  small digits at 8-24 ppem. Shared helpers append GSUB feature mappings
  and render raster comparison figures with consistent HTML escaping.
  Kerning adjustment conversion and effective-pair expansion are shared
  between checks where appropriate, without repeating font-wide expansion.
  Proof pages share their feature-setting declaration; each raster figure
  embeds one PNG and uses SVG references for actual-size and 4x displays.
  Fixed-proof margins now follow the block direction, preserving the 32-em
  line measure and paragraph gaps in vertical text. Print styles release
  both main and proof overflow. Proof pages share content-hashed font-face
  declarations, and ruby raster samples follow the trial character list.
  Font artifacts are uploaded before the optional proof steps; proof
  generation or upload failures do not block a release.
  Proof steps have individual time limits. Half-width settings for tate-chu-
  yoko apply only in vertical proofs, keeping horizontal digits proportional.

2026-11-08 (hayatedayon) Dokusho UD 1.005
- The em dash — (U+2014), the minus sign −, the fractions ¼ ½ ¾ ⅓ ⅔ ⅕, the
  degree sign ° and ‼ ⁇ ⁈ ⁉ now always take BIZ UDP's glyphs, in Japanese
  text and documents and in vertical text too, as the quotation marks and
  apostrophe already did. In BIZ UD, — is half width, so —— was only 1 em
  long; − had a shorter bar than + and =; the fractions and ‼ were squeezed
  into half width; and ° was full width, leaving a gap in 35°C. The horizontal
  bar ― (U+2015) was full width already and stays as it was.
- Added the double prime quotation mark 〞 (U+301E): 〟 raised to the height
  of 〝, with the vertical form of 〟 in vertical text, and halt and vhal as
  a closing bracket.
- Added chws and vchw, which trim only adjacent brackets and punctuation, so
  that the trimming of text-spacing-trim is available where that property
  is not. Chromium, which uses them in place of its own trimming when a font
  has them, sets the same widths as before.
- More Latin letters take their glyphs from BIZ UDP: Latin Extended-B, the
  IPA symbols and the spacing modifier letters (U+0180-02FF), Latin Extended
  Additional (U+1E00-1EFF), the Latin ligatures ﬁ and ﬂ (U+FB00-FB06), and
  the superscript ⁴ (U+2074), so 379 glyphs in all. Of these, BIZ UD set the
  pinyin letters (ǎ ǐ ǒ ǔ ǖ and so on) full width and the IPA symbols, ƒ, ǽ,
  Ḿ, ﬁ and ﬂ half width, which left gaps inside words such as nǐ hǎo. ⁴ was
  half width next to the superscripts ¹ ² ³, which already followed BIZ UDP.
  Text with decomposed accents (ǐ as i + U+030C) gets the same glyphs.
- The fractions ⅓ ⅔ ⅕ (U+2150-215F) join the symbols that take BIZ UDP's
  glyphs in Latin text, as ¼ ½ ¾ already did.
- So does the minus sign − (U+2212). BIZ UD's is half width, with a bar
  shorter than those of + and =, which already followed BIZ UDP; BIZ UDP's
  matches +. All ten thin horizontal bars (hyphen-minus, low line, soft hyphen, macron, combining
  macron, en dash, em dash, horizontal bar and ⸺/⸻, overline and minus
  sign) now have no hinting in BIZ UD's glyphs and hwid forms either, as
  BIZ UD's hinting likewise erases their stroke at some sizes from 8 to 20
  ppem.
- The ligatures that start from Latin letters in ccmp and liga follow BIZ
  UDP: fi and fl become ﬁ and ﬂ, and æ, ə, ʌ, ɔ, ɑ and ɚ with a combining
  grave or acute accent, and the tone letters ˥˩, join into BIZ UDP's glyphs
  instead of BIZ UD's half-width ones. ɑ with the accents shares its glyphs
  with the Greek ὰ ά (U+1F70, U+1F71), which therefore follow BIZ UDP too,
  as in BIZ UDP itself. The ligatures of kana and the handakuten stay as in
  BIZ UD.
- The alternates of the Latin letters in aalt (as glyph panels of applications
  show them) follow the substitutions above. Up to 1.004 they still offered
  BIZ UD's sideways letters for vertical text, monospaced and used by no other
  feature, for 115 letters, and lacked BIZ UDP's sideways forms of 47 more
  letters, such as Æ and the accented letters. The other alternates
  (full-width and circled forms and so on) stay as in BIZ UD.
- hwid, which turns the full-width forms (Ａ ％ １ and so on) half width,
  gives BIZ UD's half-width glyphs again, with their hinting and their
  sideways forms for vertical text (vrt2). They are kept as new glyphs
  (named .hwid), since the Latin letters that hwid led to are now BIZ UDP's.
  Up to 1.004 hwid gave the proportional letters, so that the digits did not
  fit two to an em, and ％ came out 1 em wide, as wide as its full-width form.
- New in Dokusho UD (neither BIZ UD nor BIZ UDP has it): hwid also turns the
  proportional Latin letters, digits and symbols (A, 1, % and so on, where
  they differ from BIZ UD) into the same half-width glyphs, so that two digits
  fit in one em in tate-chu-yoko where applications apply hwid. aalt offers
  the half-width glyph next to the full-width one for the Latin letter, and
  next to the proportional one for the full-width form, so that glyph panels
  offer both the proportional A and the half-width A for Ａ.
- New in Dokusho UD: fractions. With frac, 1/2 and 10/25 become fractions
  with small numerators, a fraction slash and small denominators on the
  baseline; the whole number of 1 1/2 and the / of and/or stay as they are
  (a reverse chaining lookup finds the digits that a slash follows). Text
  with ⁄ (U+2044) is set the same way through numr and dnom, which shaping
  engines apply around it. The numerators and denominators have the shapes of
  the superscripts below, with the side bearings of the regular digits
  reduced alike, and the digit right before and right after the slash gets
  its own spacing, so that the ink of every digit keeps the distance to the
  slash that BIZ UDP's precomposed fractions (¼ ½ ¾ ⅓ ⅔ ⅕) have in the
  median. The slash is BIZ UDP's fraction slash with no advance width, and
  the slashed zero (zero) also turns into the fraction, superscript and
  subscript zeros. BIZ UD and BIZ UDP had no dnom, and their frac only
  raised 1, 2 and 3, so that 1/2 had a raised denominator and 4/5 did not
  change.
- New in Dokusho UD: superscript digits from 0 to 9, also as the
  characters ⁰ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ ⁹ (U+2070, U+2075-2079), and in sups. The original
  fonts have only ¹ ² ³ ⁴ (BIZ UDP's, kept as they are). The new ones are the
  regular digits reduced by the ratio, and emboldened by the stroke weight,
  measured between 1 2 3 4 and ¹ ² ³ ⁴ (reduced alone, their strokes would be
  thinner), and carry no hinting.
- New in Dokusho UD: the superscript signs ⁺ ⁻ ⁼ ⁽ ⁾ (U+207A-207E), for
  10⁻³ and the like, made from + − = ( ) with the same reduction, weight
  and rise as the superscript digits, so that they keep their height
  relative to the digits (⁻ from BIZ UDP's −, as long as +). sups also
  turns + - − = ( ) into them.
- New in Dokusho UD: the subscript digits and signs ₀-₉ ₊ ₋ ₌ ₍ ₎
  (U+2080-208E), for H₂O and the like: the superscripts moved down until
  the bottom of ₍ meets the bottom of p, so that they stay within the line
  (usWinDescent). subs and sinf, which the fonts lacked, turn digits and
  + - − = ( ) into them.
- New in Dokusho UD: palt and vpal, the proportional widths and vertical
  advances of kana and punctuation (including the ccmp semi-voiced kana
  ligatures か゚-ト゚ and ㇷ゚) for headings and the like, taken from
  BIZ UDP, whose kana and punctuation are BIZ UD's glyphs set with other
  widths and vertical advances: the glyphs are placed and spaced as in BIZ
  UDP (their ink centered as BIZ UDP's where BIZ UDP redrew the outlines
  slightly, or shortened the stems of vertical brackets, and from each
  glyph's vertical origin in vpal). Body text stays solid.
- New characters: the two- and three-em dashes ⸺ ⸻ (U+2E3A, U+2E3B, two
  and three ― joined, with vertical forms in vert and vrt2), the
  non-breaking hyphen ‑ (U+2011, the glyph of -), the spaces U+2000-200A,
  U+202F and U+205F with the widths Unicode gives them, and the invisible
  characters U+200B, U+200C, U+200D, U+2060 and U+FEFF as empty glyphs of
  no width, which some environments otherwise draw as boxes.
- New in Dokusho UD: tabular figures (tnum). BIZ UDP's 1 is narrower than
  the other digits (1065 against 1393 in Mincho Regular), so that columns of
  figures did not line up; with tnum it is as wide as the others, with the
  same shape and the extra space split between both sides.
- New in Dokusho UD: a colon between two digits (ASCII or full-width) is
  raised to the middle of the digits (calt), as in 11:11, １１:１１ and
  0213:2004. BIZ UDP's colon sits
  between the baseline and the x-height, which suits text but looked low
  between digits; BIZ UD's half-width colon is centered on them. The raised
  colon has no hinting, which would pull it back to the baseline. So is a
  colon after Japanese (時刻:), which browsers put into the Japanese run and
  which looked low next to kanji. A colon after a Latin word (Note:) and
  colons in vertical text keep BIZ UDP's glyph.
- Combining marks sit on the letter before them again. The accents of
  U+0300-036F and the kana voiced and semi-voiced marks U+3099 and U+309A
  had widths of their own (half and full width) in the original fonts,
  which have no GPOS; HarfBuzz (Chromium, Firefox, Android) then zeroes
  their widths and places them over the letter itself, but no longer does
  so in fonts with GPOS, which Dokusho UD has had since 1.002 for halt. So
  q̃ had its tilde right of the q, and あ゙ its mark after あ. They now have
  no width, are moved left over the letter before them, and attach with
  mark (centered above or below the Latin, full-width alphanumeric, Greek
  and Cyrillic letters and their tnum/zero/hwid/fwid forms, as in моло́ко,
  and lowered under descenders) and mkmk (stacked); GDEF marks them. The
  kana marks attach where the precomposed kana (が, ぱ) have theirs, in
  vertical text too, where small kana (っ, ゃ, ㇰ and so on) move back before
  a mark so that they do not collide with it. i before a mark above (or the
  double-above ͡, even across marks below as in i̥̋) becomes the dotless ı
  (ccmp), and full-width ｉ becomes a full-width form without its dot. Letters with precomposed forms (é, が) are not affected. The
  half-width ´ ｀ ¨ of hwid, for which BIZ UD uses the glyphs of the
  combining accents, keep BIZ UD's spacing glyphs.
- OS/2 bit 32 (Superscripts And Subscripts) is set for the added characters.
- Symbols set on their own, in the DFLT script, follow the symbols in Latin
  text (in documents that are not in Japanese). Browsers set the hyphen ‐
  they add at the end of a line on its own, and it was BIZ UD's full-width
  glyph in English text, with a gap after the word; it is BIZ UDP's now.
- The quotation marks and apostrophe ‘ ’ “ ” take BIZ UDP's glyphs in
  horizontal text everywhere: in documents in Japanese too, where up to
  1.004 they kept BIZ UD's full-width glyphs, English passages included, and
  in Japanese, Greek and Cyrillic text. Browsers put the “ of 画面に“Hello”
  into the Japanese run and the ” into the Latin one, and a font cannot tell
  that “ from the one of “本当”, so substituting only within Latin text would
  leave the two marks unmatched. Vertical text takes BIZ UDP's vertical
  forms (half an em high; Chromium sets the quotation marks upright), as
  do the other symbols wherever they take BIZ UDP's glyphs (locl turns BIZ
  UD's vertical forms into BIZ UDP's, and vert and vrt2 turn the substituted
  glyphs that BIZ UD has no vertical form of). The vertical forms that BIZ
  UD shares between vert and vrt2 (‐ ‘ ’) take BIZ UDP's vert form in both.
  Apart from the symbols listed above, the others keep BIZ UD's glyphs in
  documents in Japanese.
- The soft hyphen (U+00AD) is hidden again in Latin text. Since 1.003 locl
  gave it BIZ UDP's glyph there, and HarfBuzz, which hides the soft hyphen
  and other invisible format characters, does not hide glyphs a font
  substitutes, so a word with a soft hyphen showed a wide blank in it. The
  format characters (general category Cf) keep BIZ UD's glyphs everywhere.
- The version string (name ID 5) of Dokusho UD Gothic names the versions of
  its sources correctly, BIZ UDGothic 1.05 and BIZ UDPGothic 1.051. Up to
  1.004 it said 1.06 for both, the version of the Mincho sources. The
  versions are now read from the original fonts.
- The checks now also draw, with hinting, the glyphs that the Latin letters
  are substituted with (superscripts, the slashed zero, the sideways letters
  of vertical text, the ligatures) and compare them with BIZ UDP, and check
  that hwid gives BIZ UD's half-width glyphs, also from the Latin letters,
  and that aalt offers them. They apply frac to check the fractions and the
  spacing around the slash, and compare the weight of the new superscripts
  with ¹ ² ³ ⁴. They check tnum and where the combining marks attach, and
  that no glyph reaches farther above usWinAscent or below usWinDescent
  (where Windows GDI stops drawing) than in the original fonts: ‚ „ and,
  in Mincho Bold, the Greek letters with tonos do, by 16 to 51 units, as
  in BIZ UD and BIZ UDP. scripts/overflow_page.py draws them.

2026-11-04 (hayatedayon) Dokusho UD 1.004
- The glyphs taken from BIZ UDP (the Latin letters, their GSUB targets and
  the symbols in Latin text) now carry BIZ UDP's TrueType hinting, which
  earlier versions removed. BIZ UD and BIZ UDP share the same fpgm but have
  different cvt and prep, so BIZ UDP's cvt is appended to BIZ UD's, BIZ
  UDP's prep is appended to BIZ UD's with its cvt numbers shifted by the size
  of BIZ UD's cvt, and the glyph programs from BIZ UDP get the same shift.
  The numbers to shift are found by tracing the programs: every cvt number
  is pushed by the program itself and used for nothing else.
- Seven thin horizontal bars keep no hinting, as before: the hyphen-minus,
  the low line, the soft hyphen, the macron, the en dash, the em dash and the
  overline. BIZ UDP's hinting rounds their stroke to nothing at some sizes
  (FreeType draws nothing, Windows a faint line), in BIZ UDP itself too. A
  check makes sure that hinting erases no other Latin glyph or symbol.
- Checked with FreeType (TrueType interpreter v35, grayscale and mono, and
  v40, 8 to 24 ppem): the Latin letters and the symbols in Latin text are
  drawn pixel for pixel as in BIZ UDP, and the Japanese characters as in BIZ
  UD. Up to 1.003, small Latin text on Windows looked fainter and softer than
  the Japanese around it.

2026-11-03 (hayatedayon) Dokusho UD 1.002
- Added halt and vhal (GPOS), which the original fonts lack: the alternate
  half widths of the full-width punctuation, so that text-spacing-trim in
  browsers (and applications that use these features) can remove the extra
  space between consecutive brackets and punctuation, as in printed books.
  Following JLReq, opening brackets lose the space before them, closing
  brackets, commas and full stops the space after them, and middle dots a
  quarter on each side. Only glyphs whose ink stays inside the remaining half
  get them (in vertical text, “ and ； do not). The glyphs do not change.
