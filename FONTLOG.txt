FONTLOG for Dokusho UD Mincho and Dokusho UD Gothic (読書 UD明朝・読書 UDゴシック)
==================================================================================

This file provides detailed information on the Dokusho UD fonts, following the
FONTLOG that SIL recommends for fonts under the Open Font License.

Basic Font Information
----------------------

Dokusho UD Mincho and Dokusho UD Gothic are fonts for reading books, in
horizontal and vertical text alike. They are Modified Versions of Morisawa's
BIZ UDMincho and BIZ UDGothic (SIL Open Font License 1.1).

Printed Japanese books set kana, kanji and punctuation on a full-width grid
(solid setting in the W3C Requirements for Japanese Text Layout), and Latin
letters in a proportional typeface. BIZ UD has full-width kana but monospaced
half-width Latin letters, and BIZ UDP has proportional Latin letters but also
proportional kana and punctuation, so neither sets text the way books do on its
own. Dokusho UD keeps everything of BIZ UD and takes only the Latin letters,
with their substitutions, from BIZ UDP of the same design.

ChangeLog
---------

2026-11-05 (hayatedayon) Dokusho UD 1.005
- More Latin letters take their glyphs from BIZ UDP: Latin Extended-B, the
  IPA symbols and the spacing modifier letters (U+0180-02FF), Latin Extended
  Additional (U+1E00-1EFF), the Latin ligatures ﬁ and ﬂ (U+FB00-FB06), and
  the superscript ⁴ (U+2074), so 379 glyphs in all. Of these, BIZ UD set the
  pinyin letters (ǎ ǐ ǒ ǔ ǖ and so on) full width and the IPA symbols, ƒ, ǽ,
  Ḿ, ﬁ and ﬂ half width, which left gaps inside words such as nǐ hǎo. ⁴ was
  half width next to the superscripts ¹ ² ³, which already followed BIZ UDP.
  Text with decomposed accents (ǐ as i + U+030C) gets the same glyphs.
- The fractions ⅓ ⅔ ⅕ (U+2150-215F) join the symbols that take BIZ UDP's
  glyphs in Latin text, as ¼ ½ ¾ already did.
- So does the minus sign − (U+2212). BIZ UD's is half width, with a bar
  shorter than those of + and =, which already followed BIZ UDP; BIZ UDP's
  matches +. Japanese text keeps BIZ UD's, since the macOS Japanese input
  method enters this character as the full-width minus. Like the other thin
  horizontal bars, it keeps no hinting: BIZ UDP's hinting erases its stroke
  at some sizes from 8 to 13 ppem.
- The ligatures that start from Latin letters in ccmp and liga follow BIZ
  UDP: fi and fl become ﬁ and ﬂ, and æ, ə, ʌ, ɔ, ɑ and ɚ with a combining
  grave or acute accent, and the tone letters ˥˩, join into BIZ UDP's glyphs
  instead of BIZ UD's half-width ones. ɑ with the accents shares its glyphs
  with the Greek ὰ ά (U+1F70, U+1F71), which therefore follow BIZ UDP too,
  as in BIZ UDP itself. The ligatures of kana and the handakuten stay as in
  BIZ UD.
- The alternates of the Latin letters in aalt (as glyph panels of applications
  show them) follow the substitutions above. Up to 1.004 they still offered
  BIZ UD's sideways letters for vertical text, monospaced and used by no other
  feature, for 115 letters, and lacked BIZ UDP's sideways forms of 47 more
  letters, such as Æ and the accented letters. The other alternates
  (full-width and circled forms and so on) stay as in BIZ UD.
- hwid, which turns the full-width forms (Ａ ％ １ and so on) half width,
  gives BIZ UD's half-width glyphs again, with their hinting and their
  sideways forms for vertical text (vrt2). They are kept as new glyphs
  (named .hwid), since the Latin letters that hwid led to are now BIZ UDP's.
  Up to 1.004 hwid gave the proportional letters, so that the digits did not
  fit two to an em, and ％ came out 1 em wide, as wide as its full-width form.
- New in Dokusho UD (neither BIZ UD nor BIZ UDP has it): hwid also turns the
  proportional Latin letters, digits and symbols (A, 1, % and so on, where
  they differ from BIZ UD) into the same half-width glyphs, so that two digits
  fit in one em in tate-chu-yoko where applications apply hwid. aalt offers
  the half-width glyph next to the full-width one for the Latin letter, and
  next to the proportional one for the full-width form, so that glyph panels
  offer both the proportional A and the half-width A for Ａ.
- New in Dokusho UD: fractions. With frac, 1/2 and 10/25 become fractions
  with small numerators, a fraction slash and small denominators on the
  baseline; the whole number of 1 1/2 and the / of and/or stay as they are
  (a reverse chaining lookup finds the digits that a slash follows). Text
  with ⁄ (U+2044) is set the same way through numr and dnom, which shaping
  engines apply around it. The numerators and denominators have the shapes of
  the superscripts below, with the side bearings of the regular digits
  reduced alike, and the digit right before and right after the slash gets
  its own spacing, so that the ink of every digit keeps the distance to the
  slash that BIZ UDP's precomposed fractions (¼ ½ ¾ ⅓ ⅔ ⅕) have in the
  median. The slash is BIZ UDP's fraction slash with no advance width. BIZ
  UD and BIZ UDP had no dnom, and their frac only raised 1, 2 and 3, so that
  1/2 had a raised denominator and 4/5 did not change.
- New in Dokusho UD: superscript digits from 0 to 9, also as the
  characters ⁰ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ ⁹ (U+2070, U+2075-2079), and in sups. The original
  fonts have only ¹ ² ³ ⁴ (BIZ UDP's, kept as they are). The new ones are the
  regular digits reduced by the ratio, and emboldened by the stroke weight,
  measured between 1 2 3 4 and ¹ ² ³ ⁴ (reduced alone, their strokes would be
  thinner), and carry no hinting.
- The version string (name ID 5) of Dokusho UD Gothic names the versions of
  its sources correctly, BIZ UDGothic 1.05 and BIZ UDPGothic 1.051. Up to
  1.004 it said 1.06 for both, the version of the Mincho sources. The
  versions are now read from the original fonts.
- The checks now also draw, with hinting, the glyphs that the Latin letters
  are substituted with (superscripts, the slashed zero, the sideways letters
  of vertical text, the ligatures) and compare them with BIZ UDP, and check
  that hwid gives BIZ UD's half-width glyphs, also from the Latin letters,
  and that aalt offers them. They apply frac to check the fractions and the
  spacing around the slash, and compare the weight of the new superscripts
  with ¹ ² ³ ⁴.

2026-11-04 (hayatedayon) Dokusho UD 1.004
- The glyphs taken from BIZ UDP (the Latin letters, their GSUB targets and
  the symbols in Latin text) now carry BIZ UDP's TrueType hinting, which
  earlier versions removed. BIZ UD and BIZ UDP share the same fpgm but have
  different cvt and prep, so BIZ UDP's cvt is appended to BIZ UD's, BIZ
  UDP's prep is appended to BIZ UD's with its cvt numbers shifted by the size
  of BIZ UD's cvt, and the glyph programs from BIZ UDP get the same shift.
  The numbers to shift are found by tracing the programs: every cvt number
  is pushed by the program itself and used for nothing else.
- Seven thin horizontal bars keep no hinting, as before: the hyphen-minus,
  the low line, the soft hyphen, the macron, the en dash, the em dash and the
  overline. BIZ UDP's hinting rounds their stroke to nothing at some sizes
  (FreeType draws nothing, Windows a faint line), in BIZ UDP itself too. A
  check makes sure that hinting erases no other Latin glyph or symbol.
- Checked with FreeType (TrueType interpreter v35, grayscale and mono, and
  v40, 8 to 24 ppem): the Latin letters and the symbols in Latin text are
  drawn pixel for pixel as in BIZ UDP, and the Japanese characters as in BIZ
  UD. Up to 1.003, small Latin text on Windows looked fainter and softer than
  the Japanese around it.

2026-11-04 (hayatedayon) Dokusho UD 1.003
- Symbols in Latin text now take the glyphs of BIZ UDP: the quotation marks
  and apostrophe (‘ ’ “ ”, full width in BIZ UD), the dashes, †, ©, € and the
  other characters of U+00A0-00BF, U+2010-205E, U+20A0-20C0 and
  U+2100-214F whose outline or width differs between BIZ UD and BIZ UDP,
  and × and ÷, which stay as in BIZ UD in Japanese text. The
  character map does not change: the BIZ UDP glyphs are added as new glyphs
  and substituted with locl, only in the default language system of the latn
  script. A new JAN language system of latn leaves them out, so documents in
  Japanese keep BIZ UD's full-width forms, also around English words, and
  “ ” in Japanese text stay full width as before.

2026-11-03 (hayatedayon) Dokusho UD 1.002
- Added halt and vhal (GPOS), which the original fonts lack: the alternate
  half widths of the full-width punctuation, so that text-spacing-trim in
  browsers (and applications that use these features) can remove the extra
  space between consecutive brackets and punctuation, as in printed books.
  Following JLReq, opening brackets lose the space before them, closing
  brackets, commas and full stops the space after them, and middle dots a
  quarter on each side. Only glyphs whose ink stays inside the remaining half
  get them (in vertical text, “ and ； do not). The glyphs do not change.

2026-11-03 (hayatedayon) Dokusho UD 1.001
- Built from BIZ UDMincho 1.06, BIZ UDPMincho 1.06, BIZ UDGothic 1.05 and
  BIZ UDPGothic 1.051
  (google/fonts at commit 2fe1e8c6b137fb13041a7cfca6fdcd8a910ea4dc).
- The Latin letters (U+0020-007E, U+00C0-00D6, U+00D8-00F6, U+00F8-017F) take
  their outlines, advance widths and vertical metrics from BIZ UDP. U+00D7 (×)
  and U+00F7 (÷) stay as in BIZ UD, since Japanese text uses them full width.
- The GSUB substitutions of those letters in frac, numr, sups, zero and vrt2
  follow BIZ UDP, together with the glyphs they lead to where BIZ UDP's differ.
- The TrueType instructions are removed from the glyphs taken from BIZ UDP,
  because BIZ UD and BIZ UDP have different control value tables.
- Renamed to Dokusho UD (BIZ UDMincho and BIZ UDGothic are trademarks of
  Morisawa Inc.), the trademark record removed, the fixed pitch flag cleared,
  and the digital signature dropped.
- OS/2.xAvgCharWidth is computed again from the glyphs (BIZ UDGothic has 1718
  where its glyphs give 1962:
  https://github.com/googlefonts/morisawa-biz-ud-gothic/issues/45).
- The copyright notice (name ID 0) and the license field (name ID 13) hold the
  copyright lines and the full text of OFL.txt, so that each font file carries
  the notice and the license the OFL requires, and releases contain only the
  font files.
- OFL.txt: the copyright line of BIZ UDGothic points to
  https://github.com/googlefonts/morisawa-biz-ud-gothic, as the notice inside
  the fonts does. The OFL.txt of the original distribution has the URL of BIZ
  UDMincho's repository on that line
  (https://github.com/googlefonts/morisawa-biz-ud-gothic/issues/24, with a fix
  in https://github.com/googlefonts/morisawa-biz-ud-gothic/pull/46).
- This is the first release to use. Version 1.000, published earlier the same
  day, had the same glyphs, but a pointer to the license instead of its text,
  the wrong URL above, and came as a zip with the documents; it is superseded.
